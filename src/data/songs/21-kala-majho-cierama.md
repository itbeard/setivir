## en

**«Kala majho cierama»** ("By My Terem") is a Belarusian folk lyric song, sung in villages and performed, among others, by our Rakaŭski Haściniec ensemble. A girl sits in the terem sewing a shirt — and hears young lads walking down the street with gusli, singing. Her heart breaks not from grief but from longing: she begs her mother to let her go out to listen and learn the songs.

# Заўвагі да тэксту / Notes on the lyrics

## be

## en

**Notes for readers outside the Belarusian context**

- **The song.** A Belarusian folk lyric song, alive at both ends of the tradition: village ensembles still sing it, and it has modern revivals — a 2019 single by the band Bielaruskaja Brama, choral arrangements for women's voices. Its formulaic vocabulary — the terem, the gusli — belongs to the oldest common East Slavic song layer. Some variants add a line: "from the terem onto the little porch."

- **Terem.** The tower or upper chamber of an old East Slavic house — in song convention, the maiden's quarters, where a girl of marriageable age sits guarded, sews and looks out at the world. "My new terem" is a fixed epithet of prosperity, not a street address.

- **Gusli.** The ancient East Slavic plucked psaltery, lap-held and strummed while singing — the instrument of epic singers and, in lyric songs, of courting lads. Young men strolling with gusli, "calling out the maidens," is the formula of springtime courtship: music in the street is the invitation.

- **"The fair one" (krasnaja).** In the old language *krasny* meant "beautiful" before it came to mean "red"; *krasnaja dziaŭčyna*, "the fair maiden," is the stock folk formula.

- **Sewing the shirt.** Needlework was a maiden's proof of worth and her dowry; sewing a *kašulia* (shirt) in the terem is the emblem of a girl ready for marriage — which is exactly why the music from outside is unbearable.

- **A heart that breaks from longing.** The shattered heart here is not grief but yearning: she is bursting to be out where the songs are, and begs her mother to let her go "to listen, to learn the songs." The song pictures its own transmission — this is literally how such songs passed from the street to the girl to the next generation.

- **Diminutives and rodnaja.** Nearly every noun is a tender diminutive — dear heart, little path, little songs, dear mama — and the key adjective *rodny*, "one's own by birth," attaches both to the heart and to the mother: the same untranslatable word of blood-belonging as in *rodny kraj*, "native land."
