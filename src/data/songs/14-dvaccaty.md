Песьня «Дваццаты» — гэта сустрэча музыкі і шчымлівага слова выдатнай беларускай паэткі [Насты Кудасавай](https://www.instagram.com/nastakudasava). Яе вершы — заўсёды падарожжа ўглыб, дзе кожны радок адгукаецца рэхам у душы. Наста — адзін з самых пранізьлівых галасоў сучаснай беларускай паэзіі, лаўрэатка прэміі ПЭН-цэнтра «Кніга году» і літаратурнай прэміі імя Міхася Стральцова. За ейнымі плячыма — зборнікі, якія сталі сапраўднымі падзеямі для чытачоў: «Лісьце маіх рук» (2006), «Рыбы» (2013), «Маё невымаўля» (2016), «Вясна. Вуснам цесна» (2021), «Побач» (2022), «Я працягваю» (2023). Творы Насты перакладзеныя на ўкраінскую, ангельскую, польскую, чэшскую і балгарскую мовы.

Песьня зробленая на словы [аднайменнага верша](https://www.facebook.com/nastakudasawa/posts/%D0%BC%D1%8B-%D0%BB%D1%8E%D0%B1%D1%96%D0%BB%D1%96-%D0%B7%D0%B0%D0%BC%D0%BA%D0%BD%D1%83%D1%86%D1%86%D0%B0-%D0%B4%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%B0%D0%B1-%D0%BD%D0%B5-%D1%87%D1%83%D1%86%D1%8C-%D0%B2%D1%8B%D1%86%D1%86%D1%91-%D0%BA%D1%83%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%BF%D0%B0%D1%82%D0%B0%D1%9E%D0%BC%D1%8B-%D0%BD%D0%B5-%D0%B2%D0%B5%D1%80%D1%8B%D0%BB%D1%96-%D1%9E-%D1%82%D1%80%D1%8B%D1%86%D1%86%D0%B0%D1%86%D1%8C-%D1%81%D1%91%D0%BC%D1%8B-/24916810267968475/) Насты. Сам верш трапіў да мяне дзякуючы нашай вроцлаўскай суполцы — і курсам «[Мова Нанова Уроцлаў](https://www.instagram.com/movananovauro)» у прыватнасьці. Кіраўніца курсаў Эмілія Гаўрус — вялікая аматарка беларускай паэзіі і шчодра дзеліцца гэтай любоўю з супольнасьцю. Дый сам Вроцлаў да гэтага схіляе: тут жывуць такія мэтры беларускага слова, як Андрэй Хадановіч і Ўладзімер Някляеў. ## en

The song «Дваццаты» (*Dvatstsaty* — "The Twentieth") is a meeting of music with the poignant word of the remarkable Belarusian poet [Nasta Kudasava](https://www.instagram.com/nastakudasava). Her poems are always a journey inward, where every line echoes in the soul. Nasta is one of the most piercing voices of contemporary Belarusian poetry, winner of the Belarusian PEN Centre's "Book of the Year" award and the Michaś Stralcoŭ Literary Prize. Behind her are collections that became true events for their readers: «Лісьце маіх рук» ("Leaves of My Hands", 2006), «Рыбы» ("Fish", 2013), «Маё невымаўля» ("My Unspoken", 2016), «Вясна. Вуснам цесна» ("Spring. No Space for Mouth", 2021), «Побач» ("Nearby", 2022), and «Я працягваю» ("I Continue", 2023). Her poems have been translated into Ukrainian, English, Polish, Czech, and Bulgarian.

The song is set to the words of [Nasta's poem of the same name](https://www.facebook.com/nastakudasawa/posts/%D0%BC%D1%8B-%D0%BB%D1%8E%D0%B1%D1%96%D0%BB%D1%96-%D0%B7%D0%B0%D0%BC%D0%BA%D0%BD%D1%83%D1%86%D1%86%D0%B0-%D0%B4%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%B0%D0%B1-%D0%BD%D0%B5-%D1%87%D1%83%D1%86%D1%8C-%D0%B2%D1%8B%D1%86%D1%86%D1%91-%D0%BA%D1%83%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%BF%D0%B0%D1%82%D0%B0%D1%9E%D0%BC%D1%8B-%D0%BD%D0%B5-%D0%B2%D0%B5%D1%80%D1%8B%D0%BB%D1%96-%D1%9E-%D1%82%D1%80%D1%8B%D1%86%D1%86%D0%B0%D1%86%D1%8C-%D1%81%D1%91%D0%BC%D1%8B-/24916810267968475/). The poem reached me thanks to our community in Wrocław — and in particular to the "[Mova Nanova Wrocław](https://www.instagram.com/movananovauro)" courses (the name means "Language Anew": free Belarusian-language courses that now run in many cities of the diaspora). The courses' leader, Emilia Gawrus, is a great lover of Belarusian poetry and shares that love generously with the community. And Wrocław itself inclines one that way: such masters of the Belarusian word as the poets Andrei Khadanovich and Uladzimir Nyaklyayeu live here. Through people like these, the community draws you into Belarusian poetry all by itself — and that is how Nasta Kudasava reached me. I first read the poem "Dvatstsaty" just before Freedom Day (*Дзень Волі* — March 25, the anniversary of the proclamation of Belarusian independence in 1918, which Belarusians at home and in exile mark as their day of freedom), and thought it a fitting occasion for another track. Having received Nasta's permission to use the text (Emilia, thank you for your help!), I set about generating.

The poem is shot through with Belarusian sorrow: in it, two waves of suffering collide — waves all too painfully familiar to Belarusians — the year thirty-seven and the year twenty. A Belarusian reader needs no footnote here. For everyone else: 1937 was the peak of Stalin's Great Terror, when thousands of Belarusians were executed in the Kurapaty forest on the edge of Minsk; 2020 was the year of the peaceful protests after the falsified presidential election — and of the brutal crackdown that followed. That is why the music here is untypical for Setivir: no usual electronics, but a mournful violin, guitar, and cello instead. With this song I wanted to convey the feeling of suffering and pain — and yet to keep the unbreakable Belarusian strength intact. It seems to have worked. At the very least, Nasta liked it:

# Заўвагі да тэксту / Notes on the lyrics

## be

## en

- **Kurapaty.** A pine wood on the northeastern edge of Minsk where the NKVD shot and buried its victims in 1937–1941; estimates run from tens of thousands to well over a hundred thousand people. The mass graves were publicly revealed in 1988, at the dawn of the Belarusian national revival, and the site became the country's central place of memory of Stalinist terror — never properly acknowledged by the state, its memorial crosses repeatedly bulldozed. The city has grown right up to the wood: people drive past it every day, close enough to "hear the howl."

- **"Thirty-seven."** 1937 — across the former USSR, shorthand for the peak of Stalin's Great Terror. "We did not believe in thirty-seven" means treating it as something distant and unrepeatable.

- **"Twenty."** 2020 — the falsified presidential election in Belarus and the mass protests that followed, crushed with beatings, torture in detention centers (the Akrestsina jail in Minsk became their symbol), and tens of thousands of arrests. The poem's hinge is the rhyme of the two dates: the terror people refused to look at returned to their own doors.

- **The knock.** In Soviet memory, the night-time knock on the door is the emblem of arrest. In 2020–2021 it came back literally, as security forces knocked on — or broke down — the doors of protesters. Hence "twenty came knocking."

- **The feminine voice.** The verb form *я б хацела* ("i would like") marks the speaker as a woman — a nuance English grammar cannot carry, and a resonant one in a year when women became the visible face of the Belarusian protests.
